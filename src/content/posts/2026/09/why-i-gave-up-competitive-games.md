I played Overwatch for over six years. It was part of my life throughout high school and the first half of college. I made lots of online friends, and I still keep in touch with some of them. I think that’s the greatest benefit I got from Overwatch because I’m not very good at making friends offline. In sum, Overwatch did add some color to my life.

So why did I eventually give it up? Some of you might think the reason is that I have to spend more time studying. Well, that’s only part of it, and only a small part.

First of all, I don’t want my competitiveness to control me anymore. Once I start playing a competitive game, I want to be the best player, and losing games becomes unacceptable to me. But trying to become a top player comes at a cost. You have to spend much more time practicing than other players do. Otherwise, you can’t beat other players. So you end up spending more and more time practicing until it starts to interfere with your life.

Competitive games severely affect my mood. Because of my competitiveness, losing games makes me upset and makes me doubt myself, even though I know that how good I am at a game doesn’t really mean anything. They also make me irrational sometimes. For example, I once stayed up until 1 a.m. to complete a limited-time event, even though I hardly ever stay up that late.

So I started trying some interesting and relaxing games. One of my friends happened to recommend Honkai: Star Rail to me, and I found that this kind of game suited me perfectly. It’s not very time-consuming and has wonderful stories.

Overall, this shift isn’t because I’m busy with my studies or with other things in my life. It’s about regaining control over my life and becoming independent again.
